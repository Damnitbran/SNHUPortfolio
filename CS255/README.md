DriverPass System Analysis and Design

CS 255 System Analysis and Design, SNHU. Brandon Willis.

Project Summary

The client was DriverPass, a company that wants to help people pass their driving tests. Its owner, Liam, wanted a system that lets customers 
sign up for driving lesson packages, book and manage lessons online, and take an online practice class. The system also has to track drivers, 
cars, and the DMV’s rule changes. My job was to gather the client’s needs from the interview and design that system.

What I Did Well

I turned the client’s interview into clear, specific requirements and kept the documents in plain language. I organized the requirements so 
each one connects to something the client asked for. I also designed the Make Reservation process carefully, so a lesson always gets a driver 
and a car and is never double booked. Writing the activity, sequence, and class diagrams helped me see how those pieces fit together.

What I Would Revise

I would revise the system design document’s diagrams. The use case diagram has a lot of lines crossing, and one use case isn’t connected clearly.
The two activity diagrams are small on the page, and the class diagram would read better on its own landscape page. I would redraw the use case 
diagram by hand so every line is clear. I would also add a date to the document and a short testing plan.

Interpreting the User’s Needs

Liam’s main problem was that his current process was based on phone calls and paper, which caused double bookings and made it hard to track who
changed what. I designed the system around that. Customers can book online, the system assigns a driver and a car automatically, and every booking
is logged with who made it and when. Liam wanted different people to see different things, so I set up different roles. Considering the user’s needs
matters because a system can be technically perfect and still fail if it doesn’t solve the client’s real problem.

My Approach to Designing Software

I start by listening to the client and writing down what they need in their own terms, then turn that into requirements. After that I use diagrams
to check the design before anything gets built. In the future I would use the same steps: interview the client, list the requirements, draw the
diagrams, then review the design with the client and the developers. I would also add early testing and ask for client feedback at each stage, 
since problems found early are cheaper to fix.
